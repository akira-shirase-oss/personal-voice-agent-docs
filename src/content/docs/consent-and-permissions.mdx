title: Consent and permissions
description: How consent works in Personal Voice Agent — the token files, what revocation guarantees, and the macOS permission prompts.
---

Personal Voice Agent's "authentication" is consent: nothing is captured, inferred or acted on until
you say so, and the record of that choice lives on disk where the service — not the UI — reads it.

## The two scopes

| Scope | What it allows | State in the first release |
|---|---|---|
| `capture` | The app may open the microphone while you hold push-to-talk | Live |
| `personal_voice` | Synthesis in a personal (your own) voice — needs a usage-rights review hash | **Closed**: always off |

## Consent lives on disk

Granting writes `<data-dir>/consent.capture.granted` — a small owner-only (`0600`) regular file —
and records the grant in `consent.log`. At open the service reads the **file**, not a memory:

- only a regular file with owner read+write grants (a directory, or `0400`, or `0000`, doesn't);
- a **symlink is never followed** — the open that would read through it fails closed;
- `0000` is the tombstone: a revocation a service never read is still a revocation on restart.

## What revocation guarantees

Revoking deletes the token **before** the message can matter — the app unlinks it (or, if unlink
fails, sets the file to `0000` itself rather than through any link) so a service that never reads
your `consent` message starts up revoked anyway. Then, while the choice is "off":

- the app sends no `ptt_down` and opens no microphone;
- a `ready` or `consent_state` claiming capture is granted is answered with the revocation again;
- a token that somehow reappears `0400` can't grant — the service reads only owner read+write.

Deletion follows consent's scope honestly: `forget` with `scope: "capture"` removes every heard
transcript (every log is deleted before the fresh one is created, needing no free space) and is
answered by `forgotten` — or `error` `forget_failed` if anything could not be deleted, never a
quiet miss. These guarantees are exercised against genuinely full filesystems in the product's
tests, on both HFS+ and APFS volumes.

## The macOS prompts

Two permission dialogs are macOS's own, and the app can't grant them for you (or for any agent):

- **Microphone** — required for capture; without it push-to-talk does nothing.
- **Speech recognition** — required to turn audio into the heard transcript.

The app also never bypasses authentication with voice imitation, and the personal-voice scope
staying closed in the first release is deliberate: a voice that could be mistaken for yours needs
its own review, not a checkbox.

```sh run=session
pva_send '{"type":"consent","scope":"personal_voice","grant":true,"rights_review":"sha256:docs"}'
pva_expect
pva_stop
```

```json output=session
{"type":"notice","code":"rights_review_required","turn":null}
```

The grant is refused outright: the scope is closed in the first release, so even a well-formed
request answers with a `notice`, not a state change. (The `ls` above also shows
`consent.capture.granted` on disk — created by the live scope's grant, owner-only.)
